House rules.

Privyra is an interface for a private desk stretched between Robinhood Chain and Elysium. It is not a custodian. The interesting state lives on the chains, in your wallet, and, for a few preferences, in this browser.

The story, short enough to check

Public markets are loud because they have to be. A print that nobody can see is not a market. Private intent is the other half of the same trade: the book you would rather not nail to the door of your everyday wallet. Robinhood Chain is where the tape is being built, an Arbitrum chain aimed at tokenized shares, with ETH for gas. Elysium is a quieter Arbitrum chain that settles to HyperEVM and burns HYPE. Privyra is the desk between those rooms.

The name is from “privy,” as in admitted to a private counsel. The seal in the mark is a closed book seen edge-on: one vertical cut, a drop of wax.

What you sign

Connecting a wallet reveals an address the site can already see once you approve it. It does not hand over a key. A later transfer, swap, or crossing will be an ordinary transaction your wallet displays. Reject it and nothing happens. Privyra will not ask you to type a seed phrase. Anyone who does is not this site.

Seals

The intended private transfer follows the shape of a one-time receiving address. You publish a receiving key. A sender derives a fresh address for you and announces it. Your session recognizes the announcement and spends from that address. The permanent wallet is how you authorize the desk, not the address the payment sticks to. The announcer and the registry are unset, so the transfer page does not derive a sample address. A sample would look like a destination. It would not be one.

    Privyra AI

    The clerk in the corner, and on its own page, answers from this site. It can explain a swap, the ferry, an ink transfer, the wax tiers, and $PRIVYRA. It reads the same end-of-day closes as Markets, and the same ether spot as the strip. “Open swap” (and the other pages) navigates. It does not invent a close.

    aiEndpoint in js/config.js is empty. Leave it empty and the clerk above is the whole assistant. To use a model later, point that value at an https URL you run yourself, or at a path on this site. The page POSTs {"messages":[{"role":"user","content":"..."}],"page":"swap"} and reads reply (or message) from the JSON. It sends no Authorization header and no key. Put the model key on your server. Prices and navigation stay in the page even when an endpoint is set, so the model cannot invent a print. An optional href in the response is honored only when it is one of this site’s own pages.

    What is live, precisely

    • Live, from the Robinhood Chain public RPC: block height, gas price, average block time, the home-page tape, and native ETH balances once a wallet is connected.
    • Live, from the Elysium public RPC: the head count on the home page, and HYPE balances once a wallet is connected.
    • Live, from CoinGecko: the ETH spot price in the strip, and the same figure if you ask Privyra AI for ether. It is not an on-chain oracle. If the request fails, the answer is n/a.
    • End-of-day, from Fin-node: equity closes on the markets page and in Privyra AI, labeled with the session date. Not a live tape.
    • In the page, with no model: Privyra AI’s explanations and navigation.
    • Not live: $PRIVYRA, swaps, pools, the ferry, seals, tiers, and spread alerts.

    A note on the catalogue

    Ticker symbols are names of companies and funds. They are not a claim that a tokenized version trades on this desk. When those tokens exist, their addresses belong in the config, beside the prices, and the markets page should start saying so. Until then the page is a catalogue with a cited close or a blank.
